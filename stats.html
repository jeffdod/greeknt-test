Find where

Choose an edition, a book, or a kind of difference, and see every matching note with its page number. The strip shows where in the edition the matches fall.

    An edition not named in a note reads the same as the main text. Accents, punctuation, movable nu, final sigma, and spacing in compound words are not recorded in the apparatus. A note records only a place where at least one edition differs from the main text, so these figures describe the apparatus, not the editions as a whole. Readings appear exactly as the edition prints them. Most are printed without accents; where the edition prints a reading with accents, the accents are kept. Page numbers are the numbers printed in the book. The kind of difference is worked out automatically by comparing the printed words. A reading that does more than one thing (for example, omits one word and adds another) is listed under each kind, so the kinds overlap and should not be added together. Words are matched to their dictionary entries using the Strong’s numbers of the Robinson-Pierpont text, so “same word, different spelling or form” and “a different word” are only given when both words are recognized with certainty. Where the program could not be sure, the note is left with no label.
